# Figure 5 Caption (Journal of Hydrology Main Text)

**Figure 5 | Paired catchment–parameter associations, hydrological shifts in association strength, and model-level overlap support across calibration paradigms.**

**(a) Paired catchment–parameter associations.** Joint density distribution of all $N = 5{,}420$ paired parameter–information relationship cells (20 orthogonal information dimensions $\times$ 271 model parameters across 36 conceptual hydrological models) between independent calibration (IC, $\rho_{\mathrm{IC}}$, green horizontal axis) and attribute-constrained parameter learning (dPL, $\rho_{\mathrm{dPL}}$, purple vertical axis). Shading represents 2D hexbin density on a log scale. Reference lines include coordinate axes ($\rho = 0$, thin grey), 1:1 identity line ($y = x$, dark dashed), and intensity threshold grid lines ($x = \pm 0.20, y = \pm 0.20$, dotted grey). Geometrically precise annotations indicate that of $n = 712$ mutually strong associations ($|\rho| \ge 0.20$ in both), $97.2\%$ ($692/712$) share identical sign concordance, with only $2.8\%$ ($20/712$) displaying sign reversals; dPL-only strong associations comprise $n = 1{,}451$ cells, while IC-only strong associations account for $n = 190$ cells ($3{,}067$ cells remain weak under both paradigms). Top and right marginal sub-axes display probability densities with overlaid opposite-paradigm profiles (dashed curves) on an identical vertical density scale.

**(b) Paired association-strength shifts across catchment-information dimensions.** Expanded 20-row ridgeline density distributions of the paired association magnitude shift $\Delta|\rho| = |\rho_{\mathrm{dPL}}| - |\rho_{\mathrm{IC}}|$ across all 271 model parameter coordinates for each of the 20 orthogonal catchment information dimensions (right-aligned labels). Alternating subtle grey and white background bands demarcate five physical catchment domains (domain titles on the right): Climate & Snow (5 dimensions), Soil & Terrain (5 dimensions), Vegetation (4 dimensions), Geology (5 dimensions), and Topography (1 dimension). Ridgelines utilize a continuous progressive diverging colormap transitioning from green ($\Delta|\rho| < 0$, stronger in IC) to light neutral grey near zero to purple ($\Delta|\rho| > 0$, stronger in dPL). Circles denote dimension medians; dashed vertical line indicates zero net shift ($\Delta|\rho| = 0$). Strong positive magnitude shifts concentrate in climate aridity, precipitation seasonality, and soil moisture/texture dimensions, whereas hydrogeological and topographic dimensions display tighter distributions centered near zero.

**(c) Strong-association overlap across conceptual models.** Empirical cumulative distribution function (ECDF, thick step curve) and rug plot (individual model ticks) of the overlap enrichment ratio $E_m^{\mathrm{enrich}} = N_{m,\mathrm{both}} \,/\, E[N_{m,\mathrm{both}}]$ across all 36 conceptual hydrological models, where $E[N_{m,\mathrm{both}}] = (N_{m,\mathrm{IC}} \cdot N_{m,\mathrm{dPL}}) / N_{m,\mathrm{total}}$ represents the expected number of overlapping strong associations under independent marginal densities. The x-axis extends down to 0.80, with a light neutral background tint across $[0.8, 1.0)$ highlighting the complete absence of models below the marginal expectation baseline ($x = 1.0$, solid vertical line). All 36 models ($100\%$) strictly exceed independence ($E_m^{\mathrm{enrich}} > 1.0$), with an ensemble median enrichment of $2.00\times$ (dotted vertical line and circle marker; pooled total: 712 observed vs. $\approx 360$ expected, $1.98\times$). Anchor annotations highlight the lowest-enrichment model (*flexb*, $1.24\times$) and the single-parameter benchmark model (*collie1*†, $2.86\times$, marked with † to denote parameter-dimension degeneracy $P=1$).
